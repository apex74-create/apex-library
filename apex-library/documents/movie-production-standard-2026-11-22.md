## A. THE PRODUCTION STANDARD (standing charter)

Every house video — product demo, sales cut, signature shot — is built to this standard:

1. **Multi-point perspective.** No single flat angle. A cut shows its subject from more than one viewpoint — low angle, face level, overhead, through-environment — the way a game camera array sees, not a tripod.
2. **Photorealistic illustration, animated.** The house look is authentic photorealistic / super-photorealistic illustration WITH animation: real light, real texture, real motion. Stills alone do not ship as video; flat cartoon does not ship as a product face unless the product itself is drawn that way (see Cut B honesty note).
3. **Game-layer textures, house first.** Texture and environment layers come from video-game asset libraries so surfaces carry real material detail. Order of use: (1) house assets, (2) verified-CC0 open libraries (§C), (3) nothing else without a licence read.
4. **Show the product WORKING.** A sales video plays in with the sale and shows the thing doing its job — the fish getting caught, the face telling time, the drone lifting off. A product video that only shows a logo is not a product video.
5. **Industry standard is the floor, not the ceiling.** Peak performance of the current tooling, graded honestly (§D) — where the tooling falls short of the standard, the gap is named, never papered over.

### The VIDEO LAWS ride on every cut (standing, unchanged)
- **Talk about the application** — narration and captions describe what the product does.
- **KEEP THE SCRIM TRIM** — overlays stay minimal and tight.
- **FADE ALL SCRIM EDGES** — no hard-edged overlay boxes.
- **ENTRANCE LETTERS FULLY CLEAR** — title and caption text fully legible before motion carries it away.

### Honesty law for this line (fabrication ban applied to film)
- No stock footage passed off as house product.
- A generated illustration of a product concept is labelled an illustration — it is not footage of a physical unit.
- Every external asset carries its licence in the catalogue before it enters a cut.

## F. TOOL OUTLINE — LETTER-SCRIM ROUTINE (added Nov 22, 2026)

**Name:** Letter-Scrim Routine.
**Home:** `~/workspace/letter-scrim-routine/` — `letter_scrim.py` (the tool), `verify_scrim.py` (the bench check), `README.md`, `MEASUREMENTS.md`, `specimens/`. It lives in its own tree like the stacked-plate R&D (§E); this standard names it, the next edit calls it by path.
**Built:** Nov 22, 2026, on the owner's order — his question, in substance: *why doesn't a routine run the letters at any font and magnification for an outline and match the scrim?* Now one does.

**What it does:** takes a line of lettering and builds its scrim from the letters' own vector outline. It loads the font, shapes the line from the font's advances, extracts each glyph's true vector outlines (fontTools — the outlines the font itself carries, never a raster trace), and generates the scrim as a distance field over that contour: a solid offset band hugging the letterforms, then a fade falling to zero — following counters, notches and serifs exactly, at any font the system can load and any magnification. The letters are drawn over the scrim fully opaque and untouched. Vector in, vector out (the SVG master is the glyph path itself); raster appears only when a PNG is rendered, at final size.

**Inputs:** text · font file (TTF/OTF) · size in px (the magnification) · margin (solid band, em) · softness (fade distance, em) · scrim colour + peak alpha · letter colour.
**Outputs:** `PREFIX.svg` (vector master, scrim + letters layers, ready to seat in a stack) · `PREFIX.png` (finished plate) · `PREFIX.scrim.png` (the scrim alone, for stacking at the scrim's proper seat) · `PREFIX.mask.png` + `PREFIX.json` (verification artifacts + geometry record, including the block rectangle a lazy editor would have used).

**The law it enforces:** the lazy-editor ban, made mechanical — **no rectangular scrim behind lettering, ever**; the scrim is cut to the contour (the arc standard applied to type), its edges faded, and the letters fully clear (Video Laws: *keep the scrim trim · fade all scrim edges · entrance letters fully clear*). If a shape in the output is a rectangle, it is a bug, and the cut does not ship.

**Proof (measured on the output files, `MEASUREMENTS.md` in the tool's home):** specimens "FISH ON!" in Noto Sans Display Black at 64 px (watch size) and 640 px (poster size), and the "APEX" title card in Noto Serif Display Black at 420 px. On all three: alpha at the lazy block's four corners = **0** (the block paints 200); alpha beyond the fade reach = **0**; the O's counter dips 200 → ~125 toward its centre instead of painting flat; letter-interior purity over a busy background = **100.000%** at every size. The bench check is `verify_scrim.py` — run it on any plate the routine produces before the plate enters a cut.
