TONE FOCUS GENERATOR

A low concentration tone with a slow wobble, split for earbuds: the carrier in one ear, the carrier plus the beat offset in the other. The two together make the beat. The numbers are yours — dial them in. Nothing here is a house frequency and nothing is claimed for the tone but focus: it steadies as the lock holds and it stops when the lock is released or you leave the mode. Volume starts low; the whole range of the device is yours from there.

ABOUT — CHART DATA & HOW TO HUNT
Raise the handset. Rear camera to the sky. Slide the tri-star ring like a bubble in a glass — tilt, don't push. Settle it on a bright light and hold it there. The ring does the rest.
Chart data: open-source bright-star catalogue (Yale Bright Star Catalog class, via the public brightest-stars list), J2000, bundled in this file. A name is printed only when the pointing solution earns it. The ring is rendered, not photographed — it is always sharp. The sky behind it may breathe in and out of focus; the ring never does. That is the design: a stable anchor in a breathing field.
The arrested picture is a viewer portal: drag it anywhere on the screen and let go — it stays where you dropped it. Horizon work puts the field of interest at the top, so drag the portal down; overhead work puts the star toward the bottom, so drag it up.
The readout lives at the bottom of the screen, under the sky, in the observation window — drag it the same way, or tap its X to fold it down to the minimal strip: lock readout and the frequency wave stay. The wave strip draws the tone the generator is making — carrier plus beat, the envelope is the beat — and reads TONE OFF when the tone is off.
The drawn layer has a colour assembly, on the CHART sheet: SKY, NIGHT RED, EMBER, SPECTRAL GREEN, DAY. It recolours the chart marks, the ring, and the banner accents — never the camera picture. Red at night, because red light leaves your night vision intact; DAY when the sky is bright, dark ink with a light edge so the marks hold against it.
